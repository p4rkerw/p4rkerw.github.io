Mosaic chromosomal alterations (mCAs) increase with age and are associated with multiple diseases, yet the cell types and states that harbor these alterations remain largely unknown. We develop CHASM, a method for detecting chromosomal copy number alterations from single-cell chromatin accessibility (scATAC-seq) data. CHASM estimates a CNA-null background for each cell, providing an individualized expectation that is critical in non-neoplastic tissues. In a cohort of 99 human kidney samples spanning age and disease conditions, CHASM identifies enrichment of mCAs in injury-associated VCAM1-high proximal tubule cells and detects the age-associated emergence of mCAs in cancer-relevant genomic regions, including chromosome 3 gains and losses and chromosome 7 gain, in ostensibly normal cell populations.
